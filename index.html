The short version

Your rankings are real. The searches behind them are small.

We ran twelve searches live from McMinnville. You are in the map on nine of them and first on eight. That is the hardest position in local search to earn, and it is genuinely yours.

Then we checked what those searches are worth. Every one of your eight number-one positions sits on a phrase with very little measured volume. It is small enough that SE Ranking's database returns no figure for it. Of the three McMinnville searches that do carry measurable volume, you are third on one and absent from the other two.

That is not a reason to doubt the profile, and not a reason to disturb it. Keyword tools badly under-report how people actually phrase a local search, and the map is answering something they largely cannot see: "near me." Nationally, injury lawyer near me runs 246,000 searches a month at a $69.20 click and car accident lawyer near me 135,000 at $90. Those resolve to a map pack, and your position in it is why the phone rings. Fifty-five reviews say it works.

But it is capped by geography. A map position serves people physically near McMinnville, a town of about 34,000. To reach past it you need to appear in the ordinary results, and there your best position anywhere across those twelve searches is ninth. Never the top eight. Absent entirely on two.

The volume in your own town is in the general terms. Attorney mcminnville oregon, mcminnville lawyers and lawyers in mcminnville oregon. One cluster of roughly 210 searches a month, and by some distance the largest measurable local demand there is. These are not the wrong searches. Plenty of people who have just been hurt do not think "I need a personal injury attorney." They think "I need a lawyer," or they are not sure what kind of lawyer they need and assume any attorney in town handles it. That search is a client who has not yet worked out what to call the thing that happened to them. General-practice firms answer it. You are third on one of the three and absent from the other two.

The rest of the demand is where you cannot be seen. On the statewide and Salem-area injury terms you are either absent altogether or sitting at ninth to nineteenth which on a phone, under a map pack and an ad block, is below where anybody scrolls. This is not an argument for chasing work in Salem. It is that measurable demand exists and at present nothing you own reaches it.

So the plan is carry across then add. Almost every page is rewritten and many change address, nine keep their exact URL, the rest are renamed, merged or moved into sections, with fifty redirects carrying visitors and links from the old address to the new one. Nothing is deleted without a forwarding address and where a page has earned something, new content goes on top of the existing copy rather than replacing it.

On page length

Substance, structure and something worth reading first

We are not chasing a word count. Google's own position is that word count is not a ranking factor and recent analysis of pages cited in AI answers found more than half were under a thousand words.

What does hold up: a study of 8,186 businesses found a correlation between more substantive words and better local rankings, the researcher's phrasing was "more words does not equal better rankings; more meaningful words is a different story."

And there is a simpler argument. Someone deciding whether to phone a lawyer reads the top of the page. Google's own guidance is that a page needs real content above the fold, not just an image. Your pages should be longer because they currently answer one question and stop, not because a number says so.

How the links run between them

Down

The homepage links to every injury service and to the Trial attorney and Law firm category pages, from within the body copy, not just a menu. Each category page links down to its own services.

Up

Every page links back to the homepage in its own words. Trial attorney and Law firm services link up to their category first, then home. Blog posts link up to the service page they support.

Sideways, within a practice

An injury page links to related injury pages. It doesn't link across to the civil trial section, those are two different practices and the site should read that way.

And a menu that works

A practice areas dropdown listing every service page. Body links and menu links are not a trade-off, the menu is how people find things, the body links are how the pages support each other. You need both.

Why the menu isn't a compromise

There's a long-standing belief in SEO that a menu link "uses up" the value of a body link to the same page so important pages should be kept out of the navigation. We checked it and the evidence doesn't hold up.

Google's own position is that links in the main content carry particular weight and sitewide menu links carry less, not that menu links cancel body links. A controlled split test on a retail site found that adding body links to pages already in the main navigation lifted organic traffic to those pages by around 25%. And in July 2026 a Google search advocate told someone planning to strip a nav link for exactly this reason that he was overthinking it.

Which matters because a menu that hides your practice areas costs you the visitor. Someone who has just been hurt and can't find "car accidents" in thirty seconds leaves. No amount of internal linking structure earns that person back.

Injury services sit at the top level because personal injury is the main practice. Civil trial services nest one level down. That is deliberate, the URL structure itself signals which practice is the centre of the firm. Every one of these pages also needs at least one link from another website; wave one adds or rebuilds thirty pages and only seventeen sites currently pass you any authority at all so link building runs alongside the build rather than after it.

The keyword half is fixed. Everything before the pipe is the same shape on every page of that type so the page is unmistakably about one service in one place. Everything after it is different on every single page, no benefit phrase is reused anywhere on the site.

Why the homepage links to three services and not twenty

Where each page sends its authority

The homepage

One short block for each category on your Google profile, each linking down to the page behind it, which for you means Trial Attorney and Law Firm. Then two or three priority services linked directly.

Every other service is named here and not linked. Twenty links from the strongest page on the site divides what it can pass twenty ways. Three links concentrate it.

Category pages

Link down to their own services and up to the homepage. Never sideways to another category, an arbitration page and a dog bite page have no business pointing at each other.

Service pages

This is where the full grid of services belongs. Someone reading about a car crash is already an injury client so showing them every other injury service there is useful rather than diluting. Each links up to the homepage and laterally only to siblings in the same silo.

Which three services get the homepage links is a real decision, not a default.
